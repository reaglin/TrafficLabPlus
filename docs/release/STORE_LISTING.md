## Description  [10000]

    TrafficLab+ turns a real intersection into a traffic lab. You find the place on a map; the
    program brings in its roads, lanes, turn lanes, speeds and street names from OpenStreetMap;
    you set the traffic and the challenge; and TrafficLab+ builds a web page with a live
    simulation of it. Cars follow a real car-following model, the signals cycle through their
    phases, and every road is shaded by its Level of Service, A (flowing freely) to F (jammed).

    It is made for transportation engineering classes, and for anyone who wants to see why an
    intersection backs up and what would fix it. It is written for people new to traffic
    engineering: every step says what it is for, which things are optional, and what each
    setting means, with a full help panel on every page.

    THE PAGE IS THE LAB

    A player opens the page and sees today's traffic. They raise the demand until roads turn
    orange and red, click a road or an intersection to see its volumes and the fixes they can
    buy — turn lanes, extra lanes, signal timing, a roundabout — and build a plan within the
    budget. Run traffic test compares the plan with today's network over repeatable 15-minute
    simulations and scores it out of 100. Submit plan makes a printout of the plan, its score and
    the traffic results, ready to print or save as a PDF. The page is a single file that needs
    nothing from the internet: it plays on a phone, a tablet, a Chromebook or a classroom PC.

    REAL ROADS FROM THE MAP

    Search for a place, zoom to the intersections you want, and load the roads from
    OpenStreetMap. Choose one intersection, or up to ten along a corridor, and TrafficLab+ makes
    the study: the signals, the roundabouts, the road ends, the lanes and turn lanes, the speed
    limits and the names. Every number says where it came from — OpenStreetMap, a default, typed,
    FDOT, or the AI — so you know what to check.

    TRAFFIC FROM REAL COUNTS

    For Florida roads, TrafficLab+ looks up the Florida Department of Transportation's published
    traffic counts (AADT) and turns each into the busy hour coming in at its road end
    (AADT × K × D, with the factors yours to change). Anywhere else, type the vehicles per hour
    from any count you have.

    SET THE CHALLENGE

    Choose the budget, what each fix costs, and the demand buttons a player tests against (today,
    in ten years, a busy day). Write the introduction players read first.

    SEE IT AS YOU BUILD IT

    The page is rebuilt beside your work after every change, exactly as players will see it.
    Draw and change the network on a plan of it — add an intersection, a road, a road end; drag
    them into place — and Ctrl+Z undoes anything.

    PUBLISH, AND HAND IN A LINK

    Publish puts every study you choose on a free website on GitHub Pages, with a summary page
    linking to them all. Each study keeps its address, so a link handed in keeps working. Copy a
    link to hand in with one click. Step-by-step instructions take a student through GitHub for
    the first time.

    TWO EXAMPLES TO START FROM

    Dr. Ron Eaglin's LPGA Traffic Lab — the LPGA Boulevard corridor at I-95 in Daytona Beach,
    five signals and the freeway ramps on a $5M budget — and a simple four-way intersection on a
    $2M budget. Play them, change them, save your own copy.

    OPTIONAL AI HELP

    If you have a key for an AI provider (Claude, OpenAI, Gemini, Mistral or xAI), the AI can fill
    in what the map does not say, estimate traffic where there are no counts, write the
    challenge, and coach you through a traffic test you ran on the page. The AI only proposes: you see every
    change with its reason, and nothing goes into a study until you tick it and apply it.
    Without a key, everything else works the same.

    PRIVATE BY DESIGN

    Your studies stay on your computer. No account with us, no advertising, no tracking. The
    pages you hand out collect nothing and send nothing; a player's name is optional and stays on
    their printout. TrafficLab+ goes online only for what you ask: the map and its roads, FDOT
    counts, your AI provider, and GitHub when you publish.

    Windows 10 version 2004 or later, or Windows 11 (x64). The preview and the map use the
    Microsoft Edge WebView2 runtime, which Windows 11 and current Windows 10 already have. The map,
    the roads and FDOT counts need an internet connection. Publishing to GitHub Pages needs a free
    GitHub account. The AI features need your own key from an AI provider.
